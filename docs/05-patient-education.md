# Creating Patient-Education Summaries

Patient-education summaries explain a condition, procedure, or discharge plan in plain language that a patient or family member can understand. AI can help you rewrite clinical language into simpler words, but you must check the content for accuracy and never present it as medical advice.

## What patient-education summaries are

- Plain-language explanations of a diagnosis, medication, procedure, or self-care instruction
- Written at about a 6th to 8th grade reading level when possible
- Short, focused, and easy to scan
- Reviewed by you against the patient's actual plan of care

## Step-by-step: draft a patient-education summary

**Step 1.** Open a fresh AI chat. Set the role:

> "Rewrite the following clinical information into plain language for a patient or family member. Use short sentences and common words. Aim for a 6th to 8th grade reading level. Do not add medical advice, new treatments, or warnings I did not provide. If something is unclear, ask me instead of guessing."

**Step 2.** Paste de-identified clinical details only. No patient names, dates of birth, or room numbers.

**Step 3.** Review the draft. Check it against the real discharge instructions or provider orders. Make sure nothing was added, removed, or changed.

**Step 4.** Have the patient or family read it. Answer questions within your scope. Refer clinical questions to the provider.

**Step 5.** Document education provided in your facility-approved system, as required.

## Example: before and after

Clinical wording (from chart):
> "The patient is post-op day 1 total knee arthroplasty. Continue enoxaparin 40 mg subcutaneously daily for VTE prophylaxis. Ambulate with assistive device three times daily. Apply ice to the incision 20 minutes on, 20 minutes off. Report fever greater than 100.4°F or increased redness and drainage."

Plain-language draft (AI-assisted, reviewed by you):
> "You had knee replacement surgery yesterday. Here is how to take care of yourself:
> - Take the blood-thinner medicine once a day as directed. It helps prevent blood clots.
> - Walk with your walker or cane three times each day. Walking helps you heal.
> - Put ice on your knee for 20 minutes, then rest for 20 minutes. This helps with swelling and pain.
> - Call your care team if you have a fever over 100.4°F, or if your knee gets more red, swollen, or drains fluid."

This version is easier to understand and contains no identifying patient details.

## Safety reminders for patient education

- AI can oversimplify or leave out important warnings. Always compare the draft to the provider's orders.
- Never tell a patient to start, stop, or change a medication based on an AI summary. That is medical advice.
- If a patient asks a clinical question ("Is my pain normal?" "Should I worry about this swelling?"), refer them to the provider. Do not answer from the AI draft.
- Document what you taught and the patient's response in your facility-approved system.
