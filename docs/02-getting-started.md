# Getting Started With an AI Tool

This chapter helps you pick and set up an AI tool for drafting shift documentation. You do not need to install anything complicated. A free web-based AI chat tool is enough to get started.

## Step 1: Check your facility policy first

Before you open any AI tool, check your employer's policies on:

- Whether AI is allowed at work at all
- Whether you may use personal devices or accounts for work-related drafting
- What data you are permitted to paste into external tools (usually: nothing identifying)

If your facility does not allow AI for documentation, stop here. This manual cannot override your employer's rules.

## Step 2: Choose a tool

Good options for beginners:

- A free AI chat tool you can access in a web browser (no install required)
- Any tool your facility has already approved

You do not need to pay for anything. The free tier of most AI chat tools is enough for drafting report sheets, handoff notes, and patient-education summaries.

## Step 3: Start a fresh chat

Open a new chat session. Do not continue an old conversation that might contain earlier patient details. Starting fresh keeps each draft clean and avoids accidentally carrying information from one patient to another.

## Step 4: Give the AI a clear role

At the start of each session, tell the AI what kind of help you want. Example:

> "You are a helpful writing assistant. I will give you de-identified shift details. Please help me draft a nursing handoff note in SBAR format. Do not add medical advice. Do not invent patient details. If something is missing, ask me instead of guessing."

This sets expectations and reduces the chance the AI makes things up.

## Step 5: Paste only de-identified details

Refer to Chapter 1 (Privacy Rules). Strip out names, dates of birth, medical record numbers, room numbers, and anything else that could identify a real patient. Replace them with placeholders like "Patient A," "the patient," "earlier this week," or "age 60s."

## Step 6: Review every draft

Read the AI's output carefully. Check it against the real patient chart in your facility-approved system. AI can miss details, mix up numbers, or sound confident while being wrong. You are responsible for anything that goes into the patient record.

## Step 7: Enter the final version in your approved system

Copy the reviewed draft into your facility's EHR or paper flowsheet. Do not save AI drafts containing patient information on your personal devices or accounts.

## What good AI use looks like (example)

Safe:
- You paste: "Patient A, age 70s, post-op day 1 abdominal surgery. Vital signs stable. Ambulated with assist x2. Pain 3/10. IV fluids running. Diet advanced to regular. Needs wound check and dressing change."
- AI drafts: a clean, organized report sheet using your words, with no invented details.

Unsafe:
- You paste: "Jane Doe, DOB 03/14/1972, MRN 8849201, room 412..." (identifying information — stop and delete)
- You ask the AI: "What medication should this patient get?" (medical advice — AI cannot answer this)
