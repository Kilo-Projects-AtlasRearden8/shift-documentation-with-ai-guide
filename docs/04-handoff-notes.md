# Writing Handoff Notes With AI (SBAR Format)

Handoff happens when you hand a patient's care to another nurse at shift change, or transfer a patient to another unit. SBAR is a common, structured format:

- **S**ituation — what is happening right now
- **B**ackground — relevant history and context
- **A**ssessment — what you think is going on
- **R**ecommendation — what you recommend next

AI can help you organize your SBAR handoff clearly. It cannot assess the patient or make the clinical recommendation for you.

## Step-by-step: draft an SBAR handoff

**Step 1.** Open a fresh AI chat. Set the role:

> "Help me draft a nursing handoff in SBAR format. I will give you de-identified shift details. Organize them into Situation, Background, Assessment, and Recommendation. Use plain language. Do not invent patient details or medical advice."

**Step 2.** Paste de-identified details for each section. Example:

> Situation: Patient B, age 60s, post-op day 2 appendectomy. Stable. Pain 2/10.
> Background: Appendectomy yesterday. No complications. Vital signs stable throughout shift.
> Assessment: Incision clean, dry, intact. Ambulating well. Tolerating diet.
> Recommendation: Continue current meds. Ambulate x3 today. Discharge planning in progress.

**Step 3.** Review the AI draft. Make sure nothing was added or changed that does not match the chart.

**Step 4.** Give the handoff verbally to the oncoming nurse, using your reviewed SBAR as a guide. Document in your facility-approved system as required.

## Example: de-identified SBAR draft

```
SBAR Handoff — Patient B
S: Post-op day 2 appendectomy. Stable. Pain 2/10 on acetaminophen.
B: Appendectomy performed yesterday without complications. Vitals stable all shift.
A: Incision clean, dry, intact. Ambulating independently. Tolerating regular diet. No nausea.
R: Continue scheduled acetaminophen. Ambulate three times today. Discharge planning ongoing; review discharge instructions with patient.
```

This contains no identifying information and is safe to draft with AI.

## Safety reminders for handoff

- The receiving nurse is responsible for verifying the information against the chart — your SBAR is a summary, not a replacement for the chart
- Never hand off using only an AI-generated note; always speak with the oncoming nurse and review together
- Do not include patient names or room numbers in the AI chat
