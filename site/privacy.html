Privacy Rules: What Never Goes Into an AI Tool

This chapter is the most important one in this manual. Read it before any other chapter.

The golden rule. Never paste any information that could identify a real patient into an AI tool.

That includes:

  • Patient names (first, last, initials, nicknames)
  • Medical record numbers (MRN), account numbers, or chart numbers
  • Dates of birth, admission dates, discharge dates, or dates of service
  • Room numbers, bed numbers, or unit names combined with patient details
  • Phone numbers, addresses, email addresses
  • Social Security numbers
  • Photos of patients, patient wristbands, or patient documents
  • Any unique combination of details that could let someone figure out who the patient is

If you are unsure whether something identifies a patient, leave it out. It is always safer to use made-up details.

Why this matters

Health information that identifies a person is protected health information (PHI). In the United States, HIPAA and state laws place strict limits on how PHI can be shared. Most AI tools are not HIPAA-compliant, and anything you paste into a public AI chat may be stored, reviewed, or used to train future models. Assume that anything you type into an AI tool could become public.

Your facility may also have its own rules that are stricter than HIPAA. Facility policy comes first.

De-identify before you draft

To use AI safely, strip everything identifying out of your notes first. Replace real details with obvious placeholders:

Real detailDe-identified replacement
“Mrs. Jane Doe, room 412”“Patient A, an older adult on the med-surg unit”
“DOB 03/14/1972”Age 54 (or leave age out if it could identify them)
“MRN 8849201”Leave it out entirely
“Admitted 10/05/2026”“Admitted earlier this week”
“John Smith, CNA on 3 West”Leave names out; say “the nursing assistant”

What to do instead

  1. Write your notes with real patient details in your facility-approved system (your EHR or paper flowsheet) as you always have.
  2. Copy only de-identified details into the AI tool to help you draft wording.
  3. Copy the AI draft back out, review it against the real chart, and enter the final version into your facility-approved system.
  4. Never save AI drafts that contain patient information on your personal phone, computer, or cloud account.
If you make a mistake. If you accidentally paste identifying patient information into an AI tool:
  1. Stop. Do not paste anything else.
  2. Delete the chat if the tool allows it.
  3. Report it to your facility's privacy or compliance officer, just as you would for any other possible privacy breach.
  4. Follow your facility's incident reporting policy.
